## Summary (2026-10-21)

1. MuRIL (max-len 128, no aug) beats the splits-v2 TF-IDF baseline on test: F1 **0.918 vs 0.897** (real-only 0.906 vs 0.885), AUROC 0.986 vs 0.980. It misses 150 scams instead of 237, but raises 112 false alarms instead of 81.
2. The gain is spread over the SMS and email sources (uci_sms_spam 0.951 vs 0.914, phishing_email 0.959 vs 0.935) and the held-out attacks (Likely-scam recall 0.941 vs 0.879; real parents 0.964 vs 0.881). Job posts stay poor (F1 0.26 vs 0.21): 98% of them are cut at 128 tokens.
3. Hindi and Kannada (synthetic templates only, no val rows): recall rises, but false alarms rise more, and Kannada AUROC drops (0.878 vs 0.930). 50 of MuRIL's 112 false alarms are one benign "UPI collect request" alert template (15 rows per language) that has no benign counterpart in train. On real benign Hinglish chat MuRIL flags 0 of 47.
4. MuRIL's scores are extreme: 69% of test is below 0.01 and 27% is at 0.99 or above, so the model is overconfident at both ends (Platt slope 0.63). Brier is still lower than the baseline's (0.044 vs 0.048). With either mapping the 0-100 meter would read 0-9 or 90-100 for 94-98% of val messages. A logit mapping is proposed for the rest; it is not applied.
5. Cost: 59 ms per message on CPU (29 ms on the Mac GPU) against 2 ms, and 950 MB against 8 MB. That is fine for the app. The augmented run gets the same one command.
6. Leave-one-source-out (4 folds, 2026-10-27, section 9; AUROC drop = in-distribution minus LOSO, MuRIL vs baseline):
   - **uci_sms_spam:** MuRIL generalises better (drop 0.025 vs 0.086; LOSO AUROC 0.974 vs 0.901).
   - **india_fraud_final:** both fail (drop 0.227 vs 0.219; LOSO AUROC 0.768 vs 0.766). At the 0.5 cut-off MuRIL's benign accuracy is 0.116, so it flags almost all genuine messages in that source.
   - **synth_india:** MuRIL is worse (drop 0.059 vs 0.014; LOSO AUROC 0.875 vs 0.921).
   - The `india_fraud_final` failure is the main argument for real Indian genuine messages in splits-v3.

## Reading

**Where the gain comes from.** At the **Likely scam** level MuRIL finds more scams: recall 0.908 vs 0.854. At the **Suspicious** level the two models are level (0.917 vs 0.921 of scams flagged; 135 vs 128 scams below Suspicious). MuRIL's suspicious cut-off is very low (0.037), and the scams it misses, it misses completely: they score about 0.002. Precision is a little lower (0.929 vs 0.945). Both models use only their own val-chosen cut-offs, and none of these numbers moved a cut-off.

**Where it doesn't help.**
- `fake_job_postings`: AUROC 0.880 vs 0.891. The 10 worst misses are mostly company profiles. The EMSCAD fraud label is not visible in the first 128 tokens, and one test-only company (Novation, 13 posts) accounts for 13 misses.
- False alarms by source (MuRIL / baseline): english 71 / 58, kannada 21 / 12, hindi 7 / 0, hinglish 13 / 11.
- Without the UPI-collect template, MuRIL has 62 false alarms and the baseline 44.
- The other false alarms are mostly genuine emails that look like a phishing template (registration reconfirmation, domain renewal, mojibake, a Polish email).
- 18 come from `VAM SYSTEMS` job posts. That company's no-profile posts are all fraud and sit in train, its profile posts are benign and sit in test. This is the known company-grouping gap in BUGS.md, now with a measured cost.

**Hindi / Kannada.** These numbers come from 410 synthetic template rows, and no cut-off was chosen on either language. They show behaviour, not real-world accuracy.
- Hindi: all 7 MuRIL false alarms are the UPI-collect template; the baseline has 0.
- Kannada: 15 of MuRIL's 21 false alarms are that template; the baseline has 12, all of them that template.
- Kannada AUROC is lower than the baseline's. The translated train rows did not teach MuRIL the benign Kannada templates.
- Is "Entering your PIN will DEBIT your account" really benign? It is a warning about a collect request, and the label is arguable. Either way it is a coverage gap (BUGS.md, synth_india entry), not leakage.

**Adversarial.** MuRIL is ahead at the Likely scam level for every attack. Against its own clean parents it loses about 4 points on `char_noise` (0.961 -> 0.918); the baseline loses 3.6 (0.897 -> 0.861). At the Suspicious level the baseline catches more `char_noise` variants (0.958 vs 0.929). `url_obfuscate` measures `textprep`, not the model.

**Calibration.** Neither model's score is a probability.
- The baseline is under-confident in the middle (Platt slope 1.88): a score of 0.75 is 85% scam.
- MuRIL is over-confident at both ends. Its 0-0.1 bin (mean score 0.003) is 3.4% scam, mostly the job posts it misses completely. Its 0.9-1.0 bin (mean 0.997) is 93.5% scam.
- MuRIL's middle bins hold fewer than 12 rows each, so their scam rates are noise.
- Platt scaling fit on val lowers test Brier for both models: 0.0481 -> 0.0438 (baseline), 0.0441 -> 0.0420 (MuRIL).

**Risk meter proposal (D24, not applied).** Keep the bands and the anchors: suspicious cut-off -> 30, likely cut-off -> 70. Interpolate in logit space instead of linearly in p, with the ends at the model's 1st / 99th val percentile.
- What it changes: the number inside a band follows how far the message is past a cut-off in log-odds. Today the curve squeezes everything between 0.405 and 1 into 70-100, and most of the Suspicious band is a few hundredths of p.
- What it cannot change: MuRIL's scores really are bimodal, so most messages will still show near 0 or near 100.
- If the meter should mean something closer to a probability, the alternative is to Platt-calibrate on val first and map the calibrated score.
- Decide after the augmented run, which may score differently.

### Risk meter (D24): how the 0-100 scale would map onto these scores (proposal, not applied)

Today `risk.py` maps the score linearly through (0 -> 0, suspicious cut-off -> 30, likely cut-off -> 70, 1 -> 100). With muril's cut-offs (0.037 / 0.405) the bands keep their meaning (the cut-offs still land on 30 / 70), but inside each band the number is driven by a few hundredths of probability. The proposal keeps the same anchors and interpolates on the **logit** scale, with the ends at the 1st / 99th percentile of the model's **val** logits (-6.41 -> 0, 6.71 -> 100; p = 0.00165 / 0.99878). Everything here is fit and shown on **val**; levels are unchanged by construction (only the number inside a band moves).

## 9. Leave-one-source-out

**Rule (`cluster-strict-v1`).** For a held-out source, every row of train **and** val that sits in a near-duplicate cluster (`cluster_id`) holding a row of that source is dropped, whatever its source: the source's own rows, the translations of its parents (including the preview parents that were attached to one of its rows as a near twin, which `translation_of` alone does not find), and other sources' near copies (e.g. `ealvaradob_phishing` rows that repeat `uci_sms_spam` messages). Val is cleaned too because the best epoch and the cut-offs are chosen on it. The scored rows are the source's own val + test rows, as in `reports/sanity.md` section 5. Other sources' test rows are never loaded, so their twins there are not counted. Both the MuRIL fold and the baseline refit below use this rule; the "old rule" columns are `reports/sanity.md` section 5, which dropped only the source's own rows (and kept the translations and the other sources' twins), so they are the optimistic reference.

AUROC measures only the ranking (how often a scam scores above a genuine message), so it needs no cut-off. F1, recall and benign accuracy are measured at a cut-off, so they also move if the model's scores shift on a new source. A high LOSO AUROC with a low LOSO F1@0.5 means the ranking survived and only the cut-off moved (F1@likely, with the fold's own val-chosen cut-off, shows how much of that a re-fitted cut-off would repair, though on a truly new source there is no val to fit it on). A low LOSO AUROC is a real failure: the model cannot tell scams from genuine messages in a source it has not seen. The held-out test rows were scored again here by a different model (one that never trained on that source), and nothing was tuned on them: the best epoch and both cut-offs come from val without the source's clusters.
