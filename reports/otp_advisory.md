Nothing was saved except this file. No val message text appears below (counts only); the messages quoted in sections 1 and 3 are test inputs written in `tests/tricky_inputs.py`.

**ADVISORY** = `rules.advisory_phrases(text)` is non-empty: a send / share / forward / give / provide / tell / disclose / reveal / ask-for verb next to OTP, PIN or CVV in the same sentence, under a negation (the request rule's own negation test, `rules._negated`). Not ADVISORY, on purpose: the pronoun form ("Your OTP is 482913. Do not share it with anyone.") and the affirmative "Keep your OTP confidential".

**The cap** (`router.safety_advice_cap`, lowers Suspicious to No warning signs) applies only if ALL hold: an ADVISORY phrase; no OTP / PIN / CVV request; text level is Suspicious (never Likely scam) and so is the verdict; no link at all; no Indian mobile number (toll-free 1800 numbers are fine); no isolation cue; no urgency cue; no gambling hit.

## Interpretation
<!-- numbers: ff4a5889baf4 -->
Written 2026-10-17 from the tables above (Sonnet 5.5, high effort); a fresh session should review it.

**Why genuine warnings come out Suspicious.** Not a phrase and not a rule bug: the head cannot see negation. It is a bag of character 2-4-grams; no n-gram weighs more than about 0.34, and each score is the sum of 100-230 small terms. The same few terms recur in every flagged advisory: `your`, `you`, `ban(k)` push toward scam (+0.09 to +0.34 each), the OTP n-grams roughly cancel (`tp ` +0.15 to +0.21, ` otp` -0.08 to -0.12), and the total lands between the intercept (-1.60) and a little above zero. The five flagged advisories sit at 0.440-0.631, i.e. 0.017 to 0.208 over the Suspicious cut-off (0.423) and 0.025 or more under Likely scam (0.656). Taking only the negation out of four of them changes the score by -0.02 to -0.06: **the warning scores slightly higher than the same sentence as a request**. So no re-weighting of the OTP words would fix this; it needs a head that reads word order (MuRIL) or training data with many genuine alerts.

**How big the problem is on val.** Small. Only 14 val messages are ADVISORY (all `india_fraud_final`; 13 benign already at No warning signs, 1 benign Suspicious that has a link). Of the 200 benign val messages that come out Suspicious, 1 is ADVISORY, 12 mention a credential word, 187 do not (phishing_email 103, fake_job_postings 35): this pattern is not where val's false positives come from. The corpus simply has almost no genuine OTP advisories, which is why the problem shows on hand-written inputs and not on val. The data fix is already planned: the 625 benign parents of the translation set are OTP and bank alerts (`reports/translate_parents.md`); after the `splits-v2` rebuild the head should be retrained and this report rerun.

**The gate, and why the cap is left OFF.** All four conditions as written are met (0 scams newly missed, 4 of 5 advisories lowered, 30/30, 0 contract failures). But the first one is empty: the cap fired on 0 of 5,267 val rows, so "0 scams newly missed" would hold for a rule that does nothing, and the benefit side is the four hand-written inputs. The probe that can show the risk says: with one warning sentence pasted onto a flagged val scam, the cap clears **38 of 1,097 (3.5%)** and **32 of 1,097 (2.9%)** of them to No warning signs, mostly `phishing_email` and `fake_job_postings`. Neither "past the head's 2,000-character cut" (12 and 13 of them) nor "SMS-sized" (9 of 430 and 5 of 430 short scams are still cleared) removes it: a scam the head scores Suspicious, with no link, number, urgency or secrecy cue, is exactly what the cap lets through. No val scam contains such a sentence by itself (0 ADVISORY scam rows), so the exposure is to a scammer who knows the rule, and the cost is a safety verdict lowered on a scam. A second weakness is in the OTP rule, not the cap: `_NEG_BEFORE` lets "Don't hesitate to share your OTP" pass as negated, so the rule misses it and the cap's ADVISORY test reads it as a warning (none is Suspicious today, so none is capped yet).

**Decision left to the user.** `config.SAFETY_ADVICE_CAP = False`; the code, flag, report adapter and tests are in. Setting it to `True` is the whole change (then add decision row S1). Options: (1) enable as is and accept about 3% of pasted-warning scams being lowered; (2) keep it off until the retrained head (splits-v2 data, MuRIL) is measured on these advisories, which removes the need for the cap; (3) first harden the negation test (a negation must sit right before the verb, not up to three words away), then re-measure the cap. Recommendation: (2), with (3) if the cap is still wanted afterwards.
