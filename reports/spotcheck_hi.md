# Hindi translation spot-check (one reviewer, v1)

Recorded 2026-10-17. Nothing in `data/`, `split_map.csv` or `models/` was touched: this is a scoring and a decision, not a rebuild.

**What was checked.** `reports/spotcheck_hi.csv` holds 50 machine-translated Hindi rows (25 scam, 25 benign), drawn by `translate_parents.py finalise` (2026-10-07, commit `afa841f`) from the 615 kept Hindi translations (92 scam, 523 benign). Their text, English parent and label match `data/external/indic/translated/translated_final_2026-10-07.csv` row for row (checked 2026-10-17); the sheet was not resampled. A Hindi-reading friend answered `hi04`-`hi50` (47 rows) on three questions:

- **Q1 meaning:** does the Hindi mean the same as the English parent?
- **Q2 label:** is the label still right (a scam still reads as a scam, a benign message still as benign)?
- **Formal:** does the Hindi read formal, i.e. not like a message people actually send? Flag only; the criterion is the reviewer's.

My own answers for `hi01`-`hi03` (1 scam, 2 benign) are not available, so those three rows are not scored. The export is saved verbatim in `docs/spotcheck/hi_friend_v1.txt`.

## The 4 failures

Causes read off the table above by comparing the English parent with the Hindi.

- **hi29 (meaning):** the brand name was translated (Licious -> "शुभ", "auspicious"), and "order" became "आदेश" (a command, not a purchase order).
- **hi34 (meaning and label, the only label failure):** the payment demand ("pay Rs 999 for verification and training kit") was re-attached as a noun phrase to the hiring clause, so the Hindi no longer asks the reader to pay and the scam ask is lost.
- **hi35 (meaning):** the sender tag "-CAMS" was fused into the sentence, so the validity clause reads as "valid for -CAMS".
- **hi39 (meaning):** the clause order is scrambled, so "withdrawn" attaches to the available balance instead of the Rs 0317.

## Pattern

The four failures sit on SMS shorthand (brand names, sender tags, alert fragments), not on complete sentences. By parent source: 3 of the 4 are in `otp_sms_preview` (bank and OTP alerts, 3 of 23 rows), 1 is in `scam_ham_india` (a comma-spliced, telegraphic sentence, 1 of 12), and none is in `india_fraud_final` (short complete sentences, 0 of 12). This describes four rows; no row was coded as shorthand versus sentence, so it is a reading, not a tested effect.

## Limits

- **One reviewer, 47 rows.** No second opinion and no agreement measure. The 47 are 7.6% of the 615 kept Hindi rows; the other 568 (565 unreviewed plus the three unscored) are unchecked, and rows like the four failures may be among them. The four failed rows are still in `translated_final_2026-10-07.csv`; nothing was removed.
- **The interval is wide.** Q1's lower bound (80.1%) does not reach 90%, and neither does Q2's (88.9%). The CLAUDE.md gate (>= 90% y on both columns) is met by the point estimates only.
- **The sheet is not the pool's mix.** It was sampled 25 scam + 25 benign; the kept Hindi translations are 15.0% scam. Re-weighting the two strata to the pool (illustration only: strata of 24 and 23 rows, normal-approximation half-widths) gives Q1 about 88.3% (+/- 11.8 points), which is below 90%, Q2 about 99.4% (+/- 1.2) and formal about 26.6% (+/- 14.6) against the sheet's 38.3%. The 13/24 formal rate on scam rows against 5/23 on benign rows drives the difference: the scam translations are prose and are flagged formal more often. The headline numbers above are the sheet's, not the pool's.
- **`formal` is a bare flag.** The export does not say what the reviewer compared against, and one person's sense of register is not a measurement of real Hinglish SMS. The 38% figure in D20 is the sheet figure.
- **Assumption on ids.** The export carries ids only; I assume the reviewer saw the committed sheet. The four failure descriptions match the text at `hi29`, `hi34`, `hi35` and `hi39` in that sheet, which corroborates it.
- **Re-running `translate_parents.py finalise` rewrites `reports/spotcheck_hi.csv`** (and `spotcheck_kn.csv`). The sha256 of the rows file is in the block above; if it changes, the scores no longer describe the sheet the reviewer saw.

## Decision

D20 in `docs/decisions/README.md`: Hindi machine translations are accepted as training augmentation only, and are not evidence of real-Hindi performance. Any Hindi metric must be labelled "translated". MuRIL and `splits-v2` stay gated on the Kannada spot-check.
