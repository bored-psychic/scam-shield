### Cited Findings

**SMS / Call Log**
- Apps must be the default SMS, Phone or Assistant handler unless they fit a listed exception, and must file a Permissions Declaration Form in Play Console ("Apps that lack a Permissions Declaration Form may be removed from Google Play"). Exceptions include "Caller ID, spam detection, and/or spam blocking" (READ_SMS, RECEIVE_MMS, RECEIVE_SMS, RECEIVE_WAP_PUSH, SEND_SMS, READ_CALL_LOG, PROCESS_OUTGOING_CALLS); "Anti-SMS phishing ("smishing")" (READ_SMS, RECEIVE_MMS, RECEIVE_SMS, RECEIVE_WAP_PUSH, WRITE_SMS), which "requires a track record of significant user protection"; "SMS-based financial transactions"; "SMS-based money management"; "Call-based authentication and authorization in banking or brokerage apps" (READ_CALL_LOG, PROCESS_OUTGOING_CALLS). No exception is literally named "fraud detection" or "scam detection". Exceptions apply only when there is no alternative way to provide the core feature. The page announces a change "(effective January 27, 2027)" removing phone-call account verification as a permitted use. — [Play Console Help: Use of SMS or Call Log permission groups](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)

**Accessibility API**
- Only services "designed to help people with disabilities" may set `isAccessibilityTool`; "antivirus software, automation tools, assistants, monitoring apps, cleaners, password managers, and launchers" are listed as NOT accessibility tools. Non-tools need a separate in-app prominent disclosure with affirmative consent and an Accessibility declaration in Play Console (one selectable purpose is "Fraud prevention, security, and compliance"; the page does not say such use is automatically approved). New wording on the 2026 page: "Any use of the Accessibility API that enables an app to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions or decisions is strictly prohibited"; deterministic rule-based automation is not prohibited. — [Play Console Help: Use of the AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)

**Call screening role (CallScreeningService), from the AOSP source Javadoc (main branch)**
- "Telecom will bind to a single app chosen by the user" that fills ROLE_CALL_SCREENING "when incoming calls are received (prior to ringing)"; it can also "see outgoing calls for the purpose of providing caller ID services". The app "must call respondToCall(...) within 5 seconds" or the framework unbinds. `Call.Details` only carries call direction, caller-number verification status, connect time, creation time and the handle (number). Only `tel:` calls are passed; only calls "not in the user's contacts are passed for screening" unless READ_CONTACTS is granted; restricted / unknown / payphone presentations are not provided. Actions: disallow, reject, silence, skip notification (skip call log only for carrier / system apps). No audio, no transcript, no information about VoIP calls. — [AOSP CallScreeningService.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/telecomm/java/android/telecom/CallScreeningService.java)

**Detecting a VoIP (e.g. WhatsApp) call**
- AudioManager's `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION` is the documented mode for VoIP / audio-video chat, separate from `MODE_IN_CALL` (carrier); the mode is a system-wide value and an AOSP commit tells VoIP apps to set it while calls are active, so any VoIP app (WhatsApp, Meet, game voice chat) can trigger it and the signal cannot say which app. A public Flutter plugin (`call_mode`) and a B4X forum recipe treat `MODE_IN_CALL` or `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION` as "call in progress". `AudioManager.OnModeChangedListener` exists (API level to confirm, believed 31). (Search-result summary; the reference text itself could not be read, see Gaps.) — [AudioManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioManager); [pub.dev call_mode](https://pub.dev/packages/call_mode); [B4X forum: check if a phone call is in progress](https://b4x.com/android/forum/threads/check-if-a-phone-call-is-in-progress.111252/)
- Android's own guidance says third-party calling apps should implement a self-managed ConnectionService and "typically rely on Telephony APIs such as the PHONE_STATE broadcast" to co-exist with carrier calls; nothing gives one app a general way to watch another app's VoIP calls. A Kotlin library for call state says detection "is best effort, especially for third-party VoIP". — [AOSP: Support third-party calling apps](https://source.android.com/docs/core/connect/third-party-call-apps); [klibs.io callstate](https://klibs.io/project/nsi-cyber/callstate)

**Foreground app**
- `UsageEvents.Event.ACTIVITY_RESUMED` (API 29) reports an activity coming to the foreground with package and class name; reading usage events needs the special PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS ("Usage access") grant from Settings. A Google issue-tracker thread reports wrong aggregated results from `queryAndAggregateUsageStats` (closed won't-fix). — [UsageEvents.Event reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageEvents.Event); [Issue 118564471](https://issuetracker.google.com/issues/118564471)

**Background launches, overlays and foreground services**
- Background activity launches have been restricted since Android 10; exceptions include a visible window, a system-sent PendingIntent (e.g. notification tap) and "The app has the `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` permission granted by the user". Android 14 / 15 added sender / creator opt-ins for PendingIntent BAL privileges; Android 17 (API 37+) adds a sender opt-in for `IntentSender.sendIntent()` and task-hijacking protection. — [Android Developers: Restrictions on starting activities from the background](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/background-starts)
- Foreground-service types: `phoneCall` requires `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS` or the default-dialer role; `shortService` runs about 3 minutes; `specialUse` needs a free-form use-case property that "is reviewed when you submit your app in the Google Play Console", and apps targeting Android 14+ must declare their FGS types under Policy > App content. — [Android Developers: Foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)

**Sideloading friction on the device itself (restricted settings)**
- Android 13 "restricted settings": apps installed outside session-based installers (browser downloads, file managers) cannot be granted Accessibility or Notification-listener access until the user opens App info and chooses "Allow restricted settings". Android 15 (per its CDD as reported by 9to5Google) extends the list to device admin, display over other apps, usage access, the default dialer / SMS roles and the SMS permission. (Press reports, 2022 and Sept 2024; not checked against the CDD text.) — [9to5Google, 12 Sep 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/09/12/android-15-sideloaded-apps-restrictions/); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098)

**Notification listener and screen-share changes in Android 15**
- "Android will stop untrusted apps that implement a NotificationListenerService from reading unredacted content from notifications where an OTP has been detected." During screen sharing, notification content is hidden, password fields are hidden from remote viewers, and "Activities from apps that post notifications during screenshare where an OTP has been detected will be hidden." Android 15 QPR1+ shows a large status-bar chip during any screen projection and stops projection when the screen locks. — [Android 15 behavior changes, all apps](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all)

**Google's own in-call protections (what Android already does, and where)**
- Android 16 (announced 13 May 2025): blocks first-time sideloading from a browser / messaging app and blocks granting Accessibility during a call; blocks turning off Play Protect during a call (Android 6+); "applied only with conversations with non-contacts", checks on-device; prompts to stop screen sharing when a call ends. The banking-app screen-share warning was piloted first in the UK (Monzo, NatWest, Revolut). The post mentions no API for third-party developers. — [Google blog: What's new in Android security and privacy 2025 (13 May 2025)](https://blog.google/security/whats-new-in-android-security-privacy-2025/)
- 3 Dec 2025: "When you launch a participating financial app while screen sharing and on a phone call with a number that is not saved in your contacts", Android warns, offers one tap to end the call and stop sharing, with "a 30-second pause period"; Android 11+; expanded to "most major UK banks", "recently launched pilots with financial apps in Brazil and India", US pilot (Cash App, JPMorganChase) from Dec 2025, now also piloting with P2P payment apps. No public enrolment API or form is described. — [Google blog, 3 Dec 2025](https://blog.google/security/android-expands-pilot-in-call-scam-protection-financial-apps/); [BleepingComputer, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-expands-in-call-scam-protection-support-to-us-bank-apps/)
- India specifics (TechCrunch, 20 Nov 2025): the screen-sharing alert pilot is with **Navi, Paytm and Google Pay**; Pixel call Scam Detection (Gemini Nano, on-device, off by default, unknown numbers only, beeps to tell participants) came to India **only on Pixel 9 and later**, in **Hindi and English**; Google is "working to bring" it to other Android phones without a timeline; Pixel share in India was under 1% in 2024 while Android is about 96% of Indian smartphones (StatCounter). Play Protect in India blocked "more than 115 million" sideload attempts "this year"; Google Pay shows "more than one million" fraud warnings a week. — [TechCrunch, 20 Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/google-steps-up-ai-scam-protection-in-india-but-gaps-remain)

**Receiving shared content (share sheet)**
- An app becomes a share target with `ACTION_SEND` / `ACTION_SEND_MULTIPLE` intent filters for `text/plain` and `image/*` and reads `EXTRA_TEXT` / `EXTRA_STREAM`; the guide lists no permission for this (the sender grants temporary URI read access; general Android behaviour, not stated on the page). For a shared screenshot that also carries a URL, read `EXTRA_TEXT` too. — [Android Developers: Receive simple data from other apps](https://developer.android.com/training/sharing/receive)
- A PWA can do the same with the manifest `share_target` member ("To accept shared files, the HTTP method must be POST, the enctype must be multipart/form-data"), but "Your PWA can only act as a web share target if it has been installed"; MDN marks it "Limited availability... not Baseline" and experimental. — [MDN: share_target](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/share_target)

## Gold-standard check: does anything already warn at the moment of a call + payment?

### Cited Findings

### Cited Findings

## Android 17 (2026) changes relevant to a third-party warning app

### Cited Findings
- Android 17 announced 16 Jun 2026 as "rolling out now" to Pixel, other devices "throughout 2026"; the consumer post names only "Improvements to Live Threat Detection" and "enhanced Advanced Protection mode" for scams and no developer APIs. — [Google blog: Android 17 features (16 Jun 2026)](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-17-features/)
